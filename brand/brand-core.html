1

The big idea

Learn the way they learned.

Arabic line — pending Gemini

In the golden age, knowledge travelled by voice. A student sat in a ḥalaqa (study circle) and heard a book read aloud from the teacher's copy, and the margins recorded who heard it, from whom, and when. Commentaries explained the text, abridgments condensed it, marginal notes argued with it, and endowments paid for the libraries, the teachers and the students' keep.

We rebuild that circle with today's tools — natural AI voices, AI explanation, and soon images and video — so a new generation learns from the same books, the same way, and walks toward the next golden age.

Audio-first is not a modern shortcut. It is the oldest way these books were passed on.

2

Audience

Arabic-speaking Muslims who want to learn from the classical books but can't get into them.

The books are long, dense, scanned, and silent. These people have the will — not the hours at a desk.

Primary · now

  • Self-directed students of knowledge (ṭullāb al-ʿilm) outside formal study
  • Professionals and parents who want turāth in the commute, the gym, the kitchen
  • Heritage learners whose spoken Arabic is stronger than their reading of classical prose

Secondary · later

  • Teachers and study circles building lessons
  • Masājid, schools and awqāf endowing libraries
  • Non-Arab Muslims learning through English

Not for · now

  • Fiction audiobook listeners
  • Researchers who need critical editions — Shamela and turath.io already serve them

Where colour should come from

The fix for "dull" is in the manuscripts themselves. Classical scribes wrote headings and key words in red ink; illuminators used lapis and gold. A colour that carries a meaning from the tradition is ownable — "warm brown" is not.

5

Naming brief

Name the practice, not the content.

Every competitor names what it holds — library, books, light, heritage. None names how scholars learned. That is the opening.

Must

  • One word, 2–3 syllables, easy to say and to spell after hearing it once — in Arabic and English
  • Prefer letters with one obvious Latin spelling: خ ع ح ق ذ split into many (Khabeer / Khabir / Kabeer)
  • Rooted in the learning tradition, but wide enough for images and video later — not literally "audio" or "read"
  • Similar length as a wordmark in Arabic and Latin script
  • Available: usable domain (.com ideal; .app / .ai fine), no same-name product in edtech or Arabic AI, open handles

Must not

  • Generic descriptors: "-AI", ".ai" in the name, "-ify", "Smart-", Reader / Book / Notebook
  • Crowded words: kitāb, maktaba, turāth, iqraʾ, Noor
  • Coined syllables with no meaning in Arabic
  • A divine name, or a revered person's name as a product label — your call where the line sits

Your candidate: Khabeer خبير

Works

  • Arabic, short, easy to say
  • Fits "ask the book"

Doesn't

  • A common noun — "expert" — so it is everywhere and hard to own
  • Puts the AI as the authority; in the tradition the text and the teacher are the authority and the learner is the hero
  • Says "expert", not "learning" or "journey" — the big idea doesn't live in it
  • Al-Khabīr (الخبير) is one of the 99 names of God — some of the audience may object
  • Three Latin spellings in the wild: Khabeer, Khabir, Kabeer

6

Where the gap is

  1. Nobody turns classical Arabic books into AI-voiced audio.

    Shamela, turath.io, Usul and Waqfeya are read-and-search only. Arabic audiobook apps (Storytel, Audible, Abjjad, Masmu3, Kitab Sawti, AlRawi in Bahrain, Rawi in Qatar) sell modern books read by human narrators. Generic Arabic AI voice tools (Nabarati, Sawt) speak text you paste in — no scans, no classical books, no questions. The one Islamic audiobook app found is in English translation.

  2. Nobody starts from the scanned PDF.

    Waqfeya is full of scanned books nobody can listen to; Usul works only on text that is already digitised. No "upload a scan, then listen" flow exists.

  3. Nobody closes the loop.

    Listen, see the real page, ask with citations, take notes. Usul and Ansari give cited answers with no audio; Tarteel is Quran only; Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM, renamed July 2026) does audio and video but is generic and not built for classical Arabic.

  4. Nobody tells the story.

    Competitors describe their shelves. None claims the scholars' way of learning.
