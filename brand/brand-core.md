## 1. The big idea

**Learn the way they learned.**

In the golden age, knowledge travelled by voice. A student sat in a *ḥalaqa* (study circle) and heard a book
read aloud from the teacher's copy, and the margins recorded who heard it, from whom, and when. Commentaries
explained the text, abridgments condensed it, marginal notes argued with it, and endowments paid for the
libraries, the teachers and the students' keep.

We rebuild that circle with today's tools — natural AI voices, AI explanation, and soon images and video — so a
new generation learns from the same books, the same way, and walks toward the next golden age.

Audio-first is not a modern shortcut. It is the oldest way these books were passed on.

### Every feature has an ancestor

| Classical practice | What it was | In the product | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Samāʿ* (سماع) | the book read aloud and heard in the circle | AI narration, voice personas | now |
| *Suʾāl* in the *majlis* | asking the teacher about the text | ask the book (AI chat) | now |
| *Sharḥ* (شرح) | commentary that explains the base text | Explain a selected passage | now |
| *Ḥāshiya* (حاشية) | the gloss in the margin | notes on the page | now |
| *Mukhtaṣar* (مختصر) | the abridgment | summarise a passage; whole-book summaries | passage now, book later |
| Illustrated manuscripts | al-Jazarī's machines, al-Ṣūfī's star charts | AI images from the book | next |
| *Dars* (درس) | the lesson | AI video lessons and tutorials | later |
| *Waqf* (وقف) | endowments that paid for libraries, teachers and students' keep | donate your recordings to every listener (pay it forward) | in the billing design |
| *Isnād* (إسناد) | every text traced back to its source | every AI answer cites its page | principle — to build |

The last row is a product rule as much as a brand one: **nothing is said without its source.**

### Claims we can make — and how (fact-checked 2026-10-16)

| Claim | Verdict | Copy rule |
|---|---|---|
| Books were passed on by *samāʿ*; manuscripts carry hearing certificates | true | use freely |
| *Ijāza* licensed a student to transmit a text, in a chain back to the author | true | use freely |
| *Sharḥ*, *ḥāshiya*, *mukhtaṣar* layered on a base text (*matn*) | true | don't use *taʿlīqa* — no source found |
| Bayt al-Ḥikma was a centre of translation and scholarship | true as worded | never "university" or "academy" — scholars dispute it was an institution |
| Illustrated scientific manuscripts (al-Jazarī, al-Ṣūfī, al-Zahrāwī) | true | no "oldest" or exact dates without a fresh source |
| *Waqf* funded learning | partly | it paid for madrasas, teachers and students' keep — don't say "education was free for everyone" |

Sources: `My Vault/Projects/ReadAI Brand/Competitor Scan.md` §4.

## 2. Audience

**Primary (now): Arabic-speaking Muslims who want to learn from the classical books but can't get into them.**
The books are long, dense, scanned, and silent. These people have the will and not the hours at a desk.

- Self-directed students of knowledge (*ṭullāb al-ʿilm*) outside formal study
- Professionals and parents who want *turāth* in the commute, the gym, the kitchen
- Heritage learners whose spoken Arabic is stronger than their reading of classical prose

**Secondary (later):** teachers and study circles building lessons; masājid, schools and *awqāf* endowing
libraries; non-Arab Muslims learning through English.

**Not for (now):** fiction audiobook listeners; researchers who need critical editions (Shamela and turath.io
already serve them).

## 4. Personality — aimed at "dull / generic"

| Trait | This | Not that |
|---|---|---|
| **Rooted** | Draws on the tradition to move forward; real manuscripts, real practices, real names | Sepia, fake parchment, "ancient" kitsch, mosque silhouettes |
| **Curious** | The polymath's energy — astronomy beside grammar, wonder in every page | Footnote fatigue, a lecture hall, a dry database |
| **Confident** | Speaks like a teacher who knows the text: short, clear, sure | Hype — "revolutionary", "powered by AI", exclamation marks |
| **Generous** | Knowledge as *waqf*: invite, share, give forward | Preachy, moralising, guilt, paywall-first |

**Inputs for story 3 (colour and type).** The fix for "dull" is in the manuscripts themselves, not in a
generic palette: lapis blue (*lāzaward*) from illumination, vermilion — classical scribes wrote headings and key
words in **red ink** — gold leaf, saffron, oak-gall ink black, astrolabe brass. An accent colour that carries a
meaning from the tradition is ownable; "warm brown" is not.

**Already taken in this space — avoid as the lead colour:**

| Colour | Who owns it |
|---|---|
| Green with yellow | Tarteel |
| Tan / bronze | Shamela — **today's ReadAI brown sits right here**, which is part of why it reads generic |
| Orange | Abjjad, Storytel |
| Black-and-white "AI minimal" | most AI tools |

Motifs to avoid: crescents, arabesque wallpaper, domes (assumed from the category; logos were not inspected).

## 5. Naming brief (for story 2)

**Must:**

- One word, 2–3 syllables, easy for both Arabic and English speakers to say and to spell after hearing it once.
  Prefer letters with one obvious Latin spelling — خ ع ح ق ذ split into many spellings (Khabeer / Khabir / Kabeer).
- Rooted in the learning tradition, but wide enough to carry images and video later — not literally "audio" or "read".
- Similar length as a wordmark in Arabic and Latin script.
- Available: a usable domain (.com ideal; .app / .ai acceptable), no same-name product in edtech or Arabic AI,
  open social handles.

**Name the practice, not the content.** Every competitor names what it holds — library, books, light,
heritage. None names *how* scholars learned. That is the opening.

**Must not:**

- Generic descriptors: "-AI", ".ai" as part of the name, "-ify", "Smart-", Reader / Book / Notebook,
  *kitāb*, *maktaba*, *turāth*, read / *iqraʾ* (crowded), *Noor*.
- Coined syllables with no meaning in Arabic.
- A divine name, or a revered person's name used as a product label — **owner's call where the line sits**
  (see Khabeer below).

**Territories to mine:**

| Territory | Seeds |
|---|---|
| The circle | *ḥalaqa*, *majlis*, *dars* |
| The transmission | *samāʿ*, *riwāya*, *isnād*, *ijāza* |
| The craft of the book | *warrāq* (copyist-bookseller), *midād* (ink), *qalam*, *ḥāshiya* |
| The journey | *riḥla fī ṭalab al-ʿilm* (travelling for knowledge) |
| The places | Bayt al-Ḥikma, al-Qarawiyyīn, the Niẓāmiyya |
| The instruments | *asṭurlāb* (astrolabe), the *kursī* (lectern) |

### The owner's candidate: Khabeer (خبير)

| Works | Doesn't |
|---|---|
| Arabic, short, easy to say | A common noun — "expert" — so it is everywhere and hard to own |
| Fits "ask the book" | Puts the AI as the authority; in the tradition the text and the teacher are the authority and the **learner** is the hero |
| | Says "expert", not "learning" or "journey" — the big idea doesn't live in it |
| | *Al-Khabīr* (الخبير) is one of the 99 names of God — some of the audience may object to it as a product label |
| | Three Latin spellings in the wild: Khabeer, Khabir, Kabeer |

**Availability (seen 2026-10-16, no registrar or trademark check):**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Same-name AI / edtech / Islamic product | none confirmed; one Arabic edtech app "الخبير" seen, unconfirmed |
| Other uses | Khabir (Arab freelance platform, with apps), a "khabeer" legal-advice iOS app, Alkhabeer Capital (Saudi investment), Al Khabeer Network (Beirut expert network) |
| khabeer.com | for sale (GoDaddy) |
| khabeer.io | live "coming soon" page |
| khabeer.ai / khabeer.app | did not resolve — possibly free |
| Divine-name guidance | خبير without "al-" is an ordinary word; Egypt's Dar al-Ifta allows shared names for businesses with conditions; SeekersGuidance calls it "not ideal", not forbidden; nothing addresses this exact name |

Verdict: keep it on the shortlist as the benchmark, not the default. It is usable, but crowded in the Saudi
market and it names the expert, not the practice.

## 6. Where the gap is

From the competitor scan (`My Vault/Projects/ReadAI Brand/Competitor Scan.md`):

1. **Nobody turns classical Arabic books into AI-voiced audio.** Shamela, turath.io, Usul and Waqfeya are
   read-and-search only. Arabic audiobook apps (Storytel, Audible, Abjjad, Masmu3, Kitab Sawti, AlRawi in
   Bahrain, Rawi in Qatar) sell modern books read by human narrators. Generic Arabic AI voice tools (Nabarati,
   Sawt) speak text you paste in — no scans, no classical books, no questions. The one Islamic audiobook app
   found is in English translation. *(Corrected 2026-10-16 after the name screen surfaced Rawi and Nabarati.)*
2. **Nobody starts from the scanned PDF.** Waqfeya is full of scanned books nobody can listen to; Usul works only
   on text that is already digitised. No "upload a scan → listen" flow exists.
3. **Nobody closes the loop.** Listen, see the real page, ask with citations, take notes — Usul and Ansari give
   cited answers with no audio; Tarteel is Quran only; Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM, renamed July 2026)
   does audio and video but is generic and not built for classical Arabic.
4. **Nobody tells the story.** Competitors describe their shelves. None claims the scholars' way of learning.

**Positioning in one line:** the only place where the classical Arabic library is *heard* — from the original
page, with every answer traced to its source, as the scholars learned.
